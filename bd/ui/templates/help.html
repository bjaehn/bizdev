How this works

BD Signals finds Chicago-area companies that show signs of needing management consulting, ranks them, and shows exactly what it found and where.

What to do here

  1. See who ranks first. Open Analysis, the one page for reading. It shows the top three new-business companies, each named by what its signals add up to (for example new leadership and a hiring push), with its score trend and the signals behind it; a chart of whether signals are rising; everyone at Watch or above, numbered from 1; and the open job postings at the bottom.
  2. Browse every signal. On Analysis open Signals dated in the last 7 days and choose See every signal: everything found, newest first, filtered by kind and by how far back to look. Each row links to the page it came from.
  3. Prepare for one company. Search by name at the top of Analysis, or click a company in the ranking, then open its brief. The brief is a two-minute read: what its signals add up to, what happened and when, with a link to every source. Use Print / Save as PDF or Copy as text to send it to someone. A company that has not been scanned can be scanned from its brief or from Find.
  4. Track a company over time. On a company's brief choose Track this company. Analysis then shows what is new about it, and you can mark its stage and keep notes.
  5. Show the market to leadership. The extra reports (market view by industry, private equity firms, open RFPs, ask your data and the Monday card) are in the Admin list under Reports.

Words used here

Sign (signal)
One dated event found in a public source: a new executive named, a cluster of related job postings, a press release, a restructuring notice. Each one links to the page it came from.
Kinds of signs
Leadership, hiring, company events, private equity, technology, procurement, filings and financials. A company with three different kinds of signs is a stronger lead than one with three signs of the same kind. Filings and financial facts are background; leadership changes, hiring, press and company events lead.
Theme
What a company's signals add up to, named by a fixed rule, never written by a model: for example Turnaround is shown when a pressure signal (restructuring, cost or financial pressure) and a leadership change are both found. The rule is printed beside every theme, with the signals that satisfied it.
Score
A number from 0 to {{ sc.score_max }}, worked out by plain code (no model) from the signals: how strong each kind of sign is, how recent, how trustworthy its source, and a bonus of +{{ sc.stacking_bonus_per_family|round|int }} for each extra kind that agrees (up to +{{ sc.stacking_bonus_max|round|int }}). The company page shows the sum.
Bands
Hot is {{ bands.hot }} or more, Warm {{ bands.warm }} or more, Watch {{ bands.watch }} or more. Below that a company is tracked but not listed.
Client
An existing SEI client. Clients are scored like any company and tagged client in the ranking; the top three cards leave them out, as expansion rather than new business.
Unverified
A company is scanned for signals unless it is known to be out of scope (excluded, outside Chicagoland, or below the size floor). If its location or size is not confirmed it is scanned and ranked anyway and tagged unverified (on the ranked list, the top three and its own page; hover the tag for what is not confirmed). It is never scored down for it: unknown is not negative. Companies are researched a batch per update, so the tag fades as facts are confirmed.

How the evidence-based priority works

Besides the Hot, Warm and Watch score, each company has an evidence-based priority and one of five action categories. It answers where business-development attention is best spent now. It is not a probability of purchase or win, and it never rests on company size, news volume, job count, portfolio size or PE ownership. It is computed by code from the stored, sourced evidence, so the same evidence always gives the same result.

Five dimensions, with their weights: specific business need {{ opp.weights.need }}, timing and urgency {{ opp.weights.timing }}, investment evidence {{ opp.weights.investment }}, potential for external help {{ opp.weights.external_help }}, evidence quality {{ opp.weights.evidence_quality }}. Each is 0 to 100 from anchor tables (in config.yaml under opportunity_model): for example a need anchored at 90 is an explicit solicitation, 75 a named platform migration, 25 a hiring cluster, and 30 a completed acquisition (a research trigger, not proof of a need). A dimension that no evidence speaks to is unknown: it adds nothing, it is not treated as negative, and the others are not scaled up to cover it. The page shows how many dimensions have evidence. One event is counted once however many outlets repeat it.

Confidence is separate: how well supported the hypothesis is (the source, corroboration by distinct events, a stated date, verified ownership). A company can have a high priority and low confidence.

Hiring alone does not establish demand for outside help, a technology named in a job posting does not prove a migration, and an acquisition is a research trigger. Evidence that is Low confidence, unchecked, or only a holding period is listed as not used.

How fresh is it

The list is refreshed by a scheduled run ({{ schedule_note }}) or when an admin presses Update my list. Every page says the date it was scored. If the scores are more than a week old, Analysis says so.

What it does not do

  • It does not write outreach or tell you what to say. It reports what was found.
  • It does not use LinkedIn or paywalled sites, and it only shows a source link for a page it actually retrieved.
  • It never posts to Teams from this screen.
  • It cannot see anything that is not public. A quiet company may simply be one the sources do not cover.

Where the admin work is

The small Admin link at the top right opens a separate area: updating the list, adding or looking up a company, marking clients, reviewing new companies, settings and run history. Everything you read is on the analysis side.
